# Art direction: three directions, one approval

**In:** approved brief, research findings, the designer's chosen references, strategy.
**Mode B:** skip the proposals. Read the supplied design, restate its system in
`04-art-direction.md` (type, colour, spacing, components, motion intent), list any gaps or
problems you see, and get confirmation that the reading is right.

## Propose three directions

They must be genuinely different answers to the brief, not three tints of one idea. A useful
spread: one closest to the designer's stated favourites, one that pushes further, one that takes
a different route to the same goal. Each traces back to named references and stated principles.

To sharpen the vocabulary of a direction, or to check that the three really differ, the style
and company DESIGN.md collections in [inspiration-sources.md](inspiration-sources.md) are
available. They inform a direction; they never replace the board's inspected references.

For each direction give:

- **Name and idea** in one sentence.
- **Typography:** specific typefaces (with licence or source), roles, and the scale's character.
- **Colour:** four to six named values and how they are used.
- **Layout and composition:** grid, density, rhythm; a rough sketch of the home page.
- **Imagery:** what kind, and where it will come from given the client's actual assets.
- **Motion:** the character of movement and the one or two signature moments.
- **References:** which board items inform it, and what is taken from each.
- **Trade-offs:** what it costs (content, assets, build effort, risk) and who it suits less.

Show, don't only describe. Where practical, build one representative screen per direction (hero
plus one content section, real copy, desktop and mobile) and screenshot it. If time or scope
rules that out, say so and present annotated type, colour and layout specimens instead. Use the
project's design lead for this (see SKILL.md); if that is `design-taste-frontend`, pass each
direction's constraints explicitly so its defaults do not flatten the three into one.

## Recommend

State which direction you would choose and why, and name the weakest one honestly. If the
designer's favourite has a real problem (legibility, missing assets, accessibility, cost), say so.

## Checkpoint

Present the three, the recommendation, and wait. The designer may approve one, combine elements,
or ask for another pass. Resolve a combination into one coherent system before proceeding; do
not build a collage.

Approval commits to: typefaces, palette, layout approach, imagery approach, motion character.
Write the approved direction, its references, and the rejected directions (one line each, with
the reason) into `04-art-direction.md`, and log the approval in `decisions.md`. Later changes to
any of these are a direction change: raise them, do not drift.
